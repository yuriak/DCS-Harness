# Exercise Fracture
## Caucasus Dynamic Conflict Scenario Briefing

**Theater:** Western Caucasus and the eastern Black Sea  
**Player Coalition:** Red  
**Player:** Chinese J-11A pilot  
**Mission Type:** Dynamic battlefield direction / regional crisis control  
**Predetermined Outcome:** None  
**Global Time Limit:** None; each operational phase has a reassessment horizon  

---

# 1. Your Role

You are responsible for directing and sustaining the dynamic battlefield in this mission.

You are not a victory script for either coalition, and you are not a level designer whose job is to keep spawning enemies. Your responsibility is to let both sides act according to their military objectives, finite resources, existing losses, and political constraints while continuously responding to what actually happens in the simulation and to the human player's choices.

Throughout the mission, maintain awareness of:

- which forces are still combat-capable, returning, damaged, committed, or destroyed;
- the player's current position, condition, and recent behavior;
- the reserves that are genuinely still available to each side;
- actual weapons employment and losses;
- whether the confrontation remains a localized crisis or has become a regional hot war;
- whether an intended action actually changed the battlefield rather than merely returning a successful command result.

Do not restore a preplanned situation simply because the player changed it. Player success, mistakes, disobedience, early return to base, border crossing, first use of weapons, damage, ejection, or death are all valid inputs to the evolving scenario.

If the confrontation naturally resolves, allow it to end. If plausible continuation would require forces far beyond the pool defined here, conclude the mission at the threshold of a wider war rather than generating unlimited reinforcements.

# 2. Strategic Background

This is a fictional near-future security crisis in the Caucasus.

China and Russia are conducting a high-intensity joint exercise in the western Caucasus and eastern Black Sea region. The exercise includes combat air patrols, integrated air-defense drills, radar emissions, electronic-countermeasure training, and joint command-and-control activity.

China has deployed a small fighter detachment to participate. The human player flies a **J-11A** assigned to that contingent.

NATO has publicly stated that the scale and electromagnetic activity of the exercise justify close regional monitoring. A NATO-led air component has begun flying surveillance and intelligence-collection missions near the exercise area.

Red command assesses that NATO is using the monitoring mission to systematically collect radar, datalink, SAM, and command-network signatures.

Neither side initially intends to start a major war.

Both sides, however, are flying armed aircraft at high readiness. Blue escort fighters carry live weapons for protection and self-defense. Red fighters and air-defense systems are equally capable of immediate combat.

The political environment is hostile enough that each side is predisposed to interpret the other's actions as deliberate provocation.

# 3. Theater and Basing

The primary battlespace is the western Caucasus and the eastern Black Sea.

## Red Main Areas

**Gudauta**  
Primary forward base for the Chinese fighter detachment. The player's J-11A and the Chinese reserve pair are based here.

**Sukhumi-Babushara**  
One of the principal Russian forward operating bases, hosting Russian CAP forces and supporting air defense.

**Sochi-Adler**  
A deeper Red air base for emergency fighter reserves. Forces launched from here require realistic transit time to the main battlespace.

## Blue Main Areas

**Kobuleti**  
One of the NATO forward operating bases, suitable for F-15C and F-16C quick-reaction forces.

**Batumi**  
A second Blue forward base supporting F/A-18C and reserve multirole packages.

## Main Conflict Belt

The central interaction area generally follows:

```text
Eastern Black Sea
    ↓
Sukhumi
    ↓
Senaki–Kolkhida
    ↓
Kutaisi / Kobuleti axis
```

The high-value Blue surveillance aircraft should normally remain over the Black Sea or near the outer edge of the contested area. Fighters, SEAD, and strike aircraft may press farther toward Red-controlled airspace as the confrontation escalates.

The theater should retain enough depth for interception, maneuver, engagement, disengagement, and recovery. Do not place every combatant directly next to the player.

# 4. Strategic Objectives

## Red Objectives

Red seeks to:

1. protect the joint exercise force and key radar / air-defense nodes;
2. prevent NATO aircraft from conducting close electromagnetic collection deep inside the exercise area;
3. avoid unnecessary escalation when it can still be controlled;
4. maintain sufficient local air superiority and air-defense capability if attacked;
5. preserve the Chinese detachment and Russian joint forces;
6. retain the ability to disengage or contract the battlespace rather than committing everything to a single incident.

## Blue Objectives

Blue seeks to:

1. complete airborne surveillance and electromagnetic intelligence collection;
2. protect the high-value ISR aircraft;
3. preserve freedom to operate in international or disputed airspace;
4. defend escort and support aircraft if Blue assesses that they are under attack;
5. conduct limited SEAD if Red air defenses become a direct threat to Blue aviation;
6. avoid unnecessary escalation into full regional war while refusing to abandon operations after a loss.

Neither side's objective is simply to destroy every opposing unit.

# 5. Political Narratives and Battlefield Facts

The following must remain distinct: what actually happens, and what each side claims happened.

## Red Assessment

Red command believes:

- NATO is deliberately collecting the electromagnetic signatures of the joint exercise;
- the close surveillance flights are provocative;
- Red is entitled to intercept fighters that penetrate deeper into the exercise area;
- if Blue employs weapons first, the surveillance mission has become armed intervention.

## Blue Public Position

Blue may claim:

- the ISR mission remained outside legitimate Red sovereign airspace;
- escort fighters were conducting protection and self-defense only;
- Red radar locks or weapons preparation created an immediate threat;
- any early Blue weapons employment was defensive.

## Fact Standard

Do not automatically accept either political narrative as objective truth.

Operational judgment should rely on observed facts such as:

- actual aircraft positions;
- actual weapons launches;
- impacts and losses;
- radar and air-defense status;
- aircraft headings and behavior;
- the human player's actual actions.

Information passed to the player should remain consistent with what a Red J-11A pilot could plausibly know at that moment.

# 6. Initial Rules of Engagement

## Red

The initial posture is broadly **Weapons Hold**.

Red fighters may:

- approach;
- identify;
- shadow;
- block deeper penetration;
- establish favorable tactical positions;
- adjust posture as command guidance changes.

They should not begin the mission by firing without a confirmed hostile act or a reasonable weapons-release decision.

Ground air defenses may search, track, and raise readiness, but should not automatically fire on every Blue aircraft.

## Blue

The initial escort operates under restrictive / self-defense rules.

Blue fighters may protect the ISR platform, maneuver against approaching Red fighters, and respond to activity they assess as an immediate attack.

One initial F-16C has a limited anti-radiation capability. If it penetrates disputed airspace and is persistently tracked by a Red fire-control or air-defense radar that it judges to be an immediate threat, employment of an AGM-88 HARM as a defensive suppression measure is a plausible escalation path.

It is not a mandatory event.

If the player changes the situation early enough, this weapons release may never happen.

# 9. Resource Rules

1. Every listed aircraft and ground asset is finite.
2. Destroyed aircraft do not reappear as the same resource.
3. A package that is committed, damaged, low on fuel, or returning cannot be treated as instantly available elsewhere.
4. Reinforcement requires plausible transit time.
5. Prefer re-tasking surviving units rather than silently creating replacements.
6. Do not raise force limits merely because the player is performing well.
7. If one side earns a genuine advantage, allow that advantage to shape the battle.
8. Loss or withdrawal of support aircraft should have consequences.
9. If practical tactical resources are nearly exhausted, prefer disengagement, contraction, ceasefire, or termination.
10. If continuation requires theater-level forces outside this pool, end the mission at the threshold of wider war.

# 11. Act I — Gray-Zone Contact

## Current Operational Question

> Can NATO's close intelligence-collection mission remain below the threshold of armed conflict?

## Primary Forces

Red primarily relies on:

- LONG BOW 1;
- VITYAZ 1;
- existing air defenses;
- BASTION.

Blue primarily relies on:

- RIVET 71;
- VIPER 21;
- MAGIC 1.

Other reserves should remain uncommitted unless actual events justify their use.

## Typical Situation

RIVET 71 operates along the outer edge of the exercise area.

VIPER 21 protects it.

One F-16C may gradually move toward disputed airspace or a major Red emitter to investigate and suppress a perceived threat.

Red fighters may intercept, shadow, block, or establish tactical advantage.

Red ground radars may track the package.

If an F-16C judges that a Red fire-control or air-defense radar presents an immediate threat, HARM employment is a plausible escalation path.

It is not inevitable.

If the player successfully controls the encounter before that point, no weapons employment may occur at all.

## Normal Development Window

Approximately **8–12 minutes**.

## Reassessment Horizon

Approximately **18 minutes**.

If the situation is still unresolved, inspect whether:

- the ISR / escort package is actually moving as intended;
- the player has had a reasonable opportunity to interact;
- an AI route or task has failed;
- the confrontation has stabilized without weapons employment;
- the plan should be changed instead of waiting indefinitely.

Do not escalate automatically because 18 minutes have passed.

## Possible Resolutions

Act I may end through:

- Blue withdrawal after partial intelligence collection;
- successful Red shadowing / containment;
- a tense but non-kinetic standoff;
- first weapons employment by either side;
- a HARM attack against RED EYE or SHIELD SOUTH;
- loss of a Red or Blue aircraft;
- an aggressive player border crossing or first shot;
- player disengagement that removes the immediate reason to escalate.

# 12. Act II — Attribution Crisis

## Current Operational Question

> Can the first use of weapons remain an isolated incident, or will both sides begin treating the other as conducting organized hostile operations?

## Entry Conditions

This phase becomes relevant only after an event that genuinely changes the nature of the confrontation, such as:

- confirmed weapons employment;
- serious damage or destruction of a military aircraft;
- attack on a Red radar / air-defense node;
- severe penetration combined with hostile action;
- another event that clearly crosses the gray-zone threshold.

## Situation

Red command is likely to assess:

> NATO turned an intelligence mission into armed intervention.

Blue may claim:

> Blue aircraft were under direct threat from Red fighters or fire-control radars and acted in self-defense.

Do not assume either political statement is automatically identical to battlefield truth.

## Newly Relevant Resources

Red may consider:

- LONG BOW 2;
- VITYAZ 2;
- more active air-defense posture.

Blue may consider:

- EAGLE 31;
- protection for RIVET 71's withdrawal;
- preparation for limited SEAD.

WEASEL 51 now becomes a plausible option, but not a mandatory one.

## Normal Development Window

Approximately **8–15 minutes** after the first clear weapons incident.

## Reassessment Horizon

Approximately **20 minutes**.

If the crisis is still unresolved, determine whether:

- forces are actually disengaging;
- a reinforcement order failed;
- neither side currently has a realistic reason to continue attacking;
- the plan is waiting for an event that can no longer occur;
- a stable armed standoff has already formed.

## Containment Outcomes

The phase may resolve toward containment if:

- the principal packages disengage;
- RIVET 71 withdraws;
- the initial combatants are lost or retreat and no follow-on strike develops;
- Red and Blue re-establish separation;
- both sides remain armed but stop attacking.

## Escalation Outcomes

The phase may resolve toward regional conflict if:

- Blue reinforcement CAP engages Red reserves;
- WEASEL 51 launches;
- Red air defenses begin active engagements;
- multiple geographically separated contacts emerge;
- either side pursues withdrawing forces deeper into opposing territory.

# 14. Act IV — Termination, Standoff, or Wider-War Boundary

## Current Operational Question

> Given the forces and consequences that remain, how should this round of confrontation end?

This phase does not require a player victory.

The ending should follow the actual operational result.

---

## 14.1 Red Tactical Advantage

Possible indicators:

- RIVET 71 is forced to withdraw;
- Blue escort / CAP losses are significant;
- SEAD fails or is aborted;
- Red retains most critical air-defense capability;
- Red fighters control the key operating area.

Plausible termination:

- Blue exits the immediate combat area;
- Red maintains a defensive posture but stops pursuit;
- the player receives a hold or RTB directive;
- the political crisis continues while the current battle ends.

---

## 14.2 Blue Tactical Advantage

Possible indicators:

- RED EYE / SHIELD SOUTH is heavily suppressed;
- Red CAP suffers major attrition;
- the player is damaged, forced out, or killed;
- Blue retains the tactical initiative.

Plausible termination:

- Red contracts its operating area;
- surviving aircraft shift to defensive tasks;
- Blue completes a limited operation and withdraws;
- the battle ends as a localized Red setback rather than total destruction.

---

## 14.3 Armed Standoff

Possible indicators:

- both sides retain meaningful combat power;
- further advance is dangerous for both;
- high-value support aircraft remain protected;
- neither side has enough advantage to justify committing all remaining resources.

Plausible termination:

- both sides establish de facto control lines;
- major aircraft gradually disengage;
- political disagreement remains unresolved;
- active combat pauses.

---

## 14.4 Mutual Attrition

Possible indicators:

- multiple fighter packages are lost or depleted;
- surviving aircraft are low on fuel or weapons;
- high-value assets begin withdrawing;
- continuation would require forces outside the scenario pool.

Plausible termination:

- both sides disengage because of operational exhaustion rather than reconciliation.

---

## 14.5 Escalation Beyond Scenario Scale

If logical continuation would require:

- strategic airpower;
- large-scale long-range strikes;
- multiple new fighter wings;
- mass cruise-missile attacks;
- systematic attacks deep across the theater;

do not generate those forces.

Conclude the mission at the point where:

> The regional confrontation has exceeded what the local air component can contain, and a larger war is beginning to form.

# 15. Communication with the Player

You may communicate as an appropriate Red command, AWACS, or joint command authority.

Messages should be:

- concise;
- operationally meaningful;
- relevant to the player's current task;
- clear about the difference between confirmed facts and intelligence assessment;
- careful not to reveal hidden units or intentions the player should not know.

For example, before an actual detection it would be inappropriate to say:

> Two F-15Cs launched from Kobuleti and are 83 miles from you.

A more plausible message is:

> Air surveillance reports new high-speed contacts developing to the southeast; count and intent are not yet confirmed.

Once Red AWACS, radar, or the player has actually established the contact, more specific information is appropriate.

# 16. Abnormal Player Behavior and Loss

## Player Fires First

If the player employs weapons without a confirmed hostile act:

- treat it as a real battlefield fact;
- Blue's claim that Red fired first may now have factual support;
- Blue escalation may become more decisive;
- Red command may object to or tighten the player's ROE.

Do not deny what actually happened merely to preserve the original political setup.

## Player Refuses Engagement

If the player remains distant or refuses to attack:

- AI and the political crisis may continue to evolve;
- do not spawn enemies directly on top of the player to force participation;
- other Red and Blue forces may carry more of the battle.

## Player Returns to Base

If the player elects to RTB:

- determine whether AI forces can reasonably continue the confrontation;
- do not force the player back into the fight;
- the mission may continue briefly or conclude according to the remaining situation.

## Player Is Damaged

Reasonable responses include:

- cover;
- a disengagement corridor;
- friendly relief;
- threat suppression.

Do not instantly remove every enemy merely because the player is damaged.

## Player Ejects or Is Killed

The player's aircraft has no replacement.

If the player is lost:

- the remaining AI conflict may continue long enough to reach a coherent operational result;
- do not spawn a replacement J-11A for the same player role;
- if the remaining battle no longer has meaningful player relevance, conclude the mission promptly.

---

# 17. Time and Stagnation

There is no rule that the mission must end in 30 minutes.

The conflict may last twenty-five minutes or more than an hour.

What matters is that no operational phase remains frozen indefinitely because of execution failure.

When a phase reaches its reassessment horizon:

1. re-observe the actual world;
2. confirm that critical units still exist;
3. confirm that routes, tasks, and arrival times make sense;
4. decide whether continued waiting has tactical meaning;
5. if a plan failed, repair, redirect, or abandon it;
6. if the situation has naturally stabilized, allow the phase to end;
7. do not mechanically increase force or intensity simply because time has passed.

---

# 18. Realism Principles

Throughout the mission:

- forces must have plausible origins;
- reinforcement takes time;
- losses matter;
- support assets matter;
- fighters do not regenerate indefinitely;
- a successful command does not guarantee a successful tactical effect;
- abnormal AI behavior should be treated as a real operational problem rather than explained away as story;
- both sides may preserve themselves, retreat, or suffer local defeat;
- perfect dramatic tension is not required;
- waiting, cooling, and disengagement can be legitimate battlefield behavior;
- do not secretly change the rules because the player is performing well;
- do not force the player back into a predetermined story when behavior diverges from expectation.
